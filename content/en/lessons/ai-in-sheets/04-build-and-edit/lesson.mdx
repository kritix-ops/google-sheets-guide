# Building and editing sheets with Gemini

The Gemini side panel takes natural-language prompts and produces structural changes to your sheet: new scorecards above existing data, a P&L dashboard on a fresh tab, a re-layout of a buyer-vs-platform pivot. You describe the outcome; Gemini drafts the cells, formulas, formatting, and charts; you review the proposal in a side-by-side diff and then apply or discard. The April 2026 launch (the announcement is in CLAUDE.md's references) reported 70.48% on SpreadsheetBench, which means Gemini gets it right roughly seven times out of ten on complex spreadsheet tasks. The other three times are why this lesson exists.

When it works, build-and-edit replaces an afternoon of manual dashboard construction with five minutes of prompting. When it doesn't, it produces an afternoon of debugging a dashboard with three subtly wrong formulas. The difference between those two outcomes is the prompt and the review. This lesson walks through both, with the team's actual prompt patterns and the failure modes they've shipped through.

## Opening the side panel

Click the **Ask Gemini** button at the top right of the sheet (the sparkle icon next to the Share button), or use `Ctrl+Alt+G` on Windows / `Cmd+Ctrl+G` on Mac. The panel docks on the right. Type your prompt at the bottom; Gemini's proposal appears above it, with **Apply** and **Discard** buttons under the proposal pane.

The side panel is the same panel covered in lesson 5.1 ("what Gemini sees"), so the same scoping rules apply: active sheet first, then the cells you point at, then everything else nothing. If you want a build that uses data from a different tab, switch to that tab before sending the prompt or paste the data in explicitly.

## Build vs edit

The side panel handles two distinct verbs, and they want different prompt shapes:

| Verb | What it does | Prompt shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** | Creates new structure: scorecards, pivots, new tabs, charts | Describe the output: what type of artifact, what fields, what layout |
| **Edit** | Changes existing structure: formats a column, adds a year-over-year, moves a scorecard | Describe the change: what existed, what should change, what should stay |

The two require different prompts. Build prompts are weighted toward describing the output: what type of artifact, what fields, what layout. Edit prompts are weighted toward describing the delta: what existed, what should change, what should stay untouched. Mixing them ("build a scorecard and also add a chart and also change the date format on column A") gives Gemini three jobs to do, and the proposal usually nails one and bungles the other two.

## What a good build prompt looks like

Three patterns the team uses successfully:

```
Add a scorecard above the campaign log showing total spend and total revenue.
```

```
Build a buyer-vs-platform pivot to the right of the data, summing revenue.
```

```
On a new "Monthly Summary" tab, build a table grouping campaigns by month with total spend, total revenue, and ROI.
```

What makes them work:

- **Named output type.** Scorecard, pivot, table, chart. Gemini knows the shapes these come in.
- **Named fields.** "Total spend and total revenue" beats "the totals". Gemini does not guess your column names well.
- **Named location.** "Above the data", "to the right", "on a new tab". Without this, Gemini guesses, often poorly.
- **Implicit scope.** The active sheet's data is the source. If you want a different source, say so explicitly: "Use the rolled-up monthly summary on the `Daily Summary` tab."

## The proposal review pane

After you submit a build prompt, the side panel shows a side-by-side diff: the current sheet on the left, the proposed sheet on the right. The proposal highlights what Gemini would change: new cells, modified cells, new charts, deleted ranges.

The discipline is to read the proposal before applying. Not skim. Read.

Five things to check:

1. **Are the labels correct?** "Total Spend" or "Spend Total"? Spelling and casing.
2. **Are the formulas pointing at the right cells?** A `SUM(F2:F61)` is fine. A `SUM(F:F)` is wrong (unbounded). A `SUM(E2:E61)` is wrong (wrong column).
3. **Do the totals match a quick mental calculation?** If you can eyeball the column and know it's about $20K, a scorecard showing $200K is wrong.
4. **Did Gemini change cells outside the new structure?** Side effects in the campaign log itself are common and bad.
5. **Did Gemini delete anything?** Look for red-highlighted removals.

Apply commits. Discard goes back to the previous state. Iterating is fine: discard, refine the prompt, try again. Three iterations is normal for a non-trivial build. The team's rule: snapshot the workbook (named version, Track 4 lesson 10) before a high-stakes Apply, because the undo behavior after a complex multi-step build is not reliably a single step.

## What Gemini gets right

- **Standard dashboards.** Scorecard above the data, pivot to the right, a few sparkline columns. These are templates Gemini has seen a thousand times.
- **Conditional formatting on numeric columns.** Heatmap-style color scales, threshold-based rules.
- **Common formulas.** `SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `COUNTIFS`, ROI = `(revenue - spend) / spend`.
- **Reformatting numbers.** Currency, percentage, date. (Currency conventions can drift; see below.)

## What Gemini gets confidently wrong

- **Custom business logic.** "Calculate ROI weighted by media buyer seniority" produces a plausible-looking formula that is not the team's actual weighting. Domain-specific aggregations need human verification, every time.
- **Column names that don't exist.** Gemini hallucinates plausible columns. If your data has `Profit` but Gemini's proposal references `Margin`, the formula breaks silently.
- **Formatting conventions.** Currency symbols, date formats, decimal precision. The team's conventions ("MMM dd" date format, `$#,##0` currency) are not what Gemini defaults to.
- **Edge cases in pivots.** Pivots with blanks in the source. Pivots over date columns where dates are stored as text. Gemini's proposal often does not handle these.
- **IMPORTRANGE-backed dashboards.** Gemini sees the resolved values, not the import. If the upstream IMPORTRANGE is truncated to 1000 rows by mistake, Gemini's pivot is also truncated and you'd never know. Lesson 5.1 covers this; Track 1 lesson 27 covers IMPORTRANGE itself.

The mental model: Gemini is a strong junior analyst. It writes clean, conventional artifacts fast. It does not know your team's conventions or your data's quirks. Verify, every time, before applying.

## Iteration as the workflow

Three iterations is normal for non-trivial builds. The team's pattern:

1. **First prompt is the rough shape.** "Build a P&L by buyer." Gemini drafts something.
2. **First review surfaces the gap.** Wrong column, wrong layout, missing field.
3. **Second prompt is the correction.** "Move the pivot to start at column J, not column I. Add a Profit column."
4. **Third prompt is the polish.** "Format the Profit column as currency with no decimals. Highlight rows with negative profit in red."

Discarding after each iteration is free. The cost is the next prompt. Don't apply the rough draft and try to clean up; the cleanup costs more time than the next prompt does, and the undo after apply is unreliable.

Click **Start lesson** to provision the 60-row campaign log. Four cells to fill on the Campaigns tab:

- **L1** is the natural-language prompt you would type into the side panel. Include `scorecard` or `dashboard`, and `spend` or `revenue`.
- **I1, J1, I2, J2** are the scorecard cells. I1 = `Total Spend`, J1 = the sum of all 60 spends, I2 = `Total Revenue`, J2 = the sum of all 60 revenues. You can compute the values with `=SUM(F2:F61)` and `=SUM(G2:G61)` if you want; the grader only checks the resulting values, not the formulas (because Fill-style applies write literals).
- **K1** records the review discipline: a literal note that contains `review` or `verify`, and `before applying`.

You're producing the artifact and the prompt that produced it. In a real build session, the prompt comes first and Gemini fills the scorecard. Here you write both.

## Used in the wild

In the team's actual aggregator:

- **The first prompt of every monthly review session is `On a new "Monthly Summary" tab, build a table grouping campaigns by buyer with total spend, total revenue, and ROI.`** Gemini builds it in 10 seconds. Eyeball, apply, move on.
- **Iteration is the workflow.** Build → review → discard → refine → build again. Three iterations on average to land on the right artifact. The prompt history lives in a note on the tab so future builders see what worked.
- **High-stakes dashboards never apply on first build.** The team builds, exports the proposal to a comment thread, has a teammate review, then applies. The two-eye rule for anything that touches a board-level scorecard.
- **Every applied build gets a sibling note recording the prompt.** `Built by: Yoav, 2026-05-11, prompt: "Add a scorecard..."`. The note is the only audit trail of what was AI-generated and what was hand-written.

## Pro pitfalls

- **Formulas that look right but compute the wrong thing.** Gemini's `=SUMIFS(F2:F61, B2:B61, "Yoav")` is almost right; the right one is `=SUMIFS(F2:F61, B2:B61, "Yoav Cohen")`. Always spot-check 3 to 5 cells in the proposal against your own mental model.
- **Hallucinated column names.** Gemini sometimes references `Profit` when your column is `Net Revenue`. The formula evaluates to `#REF!` or zero, depending on the variant.
- **Formatting that fights team conventions.** Currency with two decimals, dates in US format, percentages with no precision. Iterate on the prompt to pin down the conventions, or fix after apply.
- **A build prompt that produces 200 cells of which 5 are wrong.** Hard to spot. Use the diff view aggressively. Sort by "modified" first if the side panel offers it.
- **The side panel can't see other tabs.** A prompt that says "use the data from the Aggregator tab" works only if Aggregator is the active tab when you send the prompt. Switching tabs mid-session is a quiet source of wrong builds.
- **Apply-then-undo doesn't always work cleanly.** Build operations are usually one undo unit, but complex multi-step builds can be partial. Save a snapshot of the workbook before high-stakes builds.
- **Recalc cost on Gemini-built pivots.** Gemini sometimes proposes a layout that uses volatile functions like `OFFSET` or `INDIRECT` (Track 1 lesson 24's slow neighbors). On a 50K-row source the dashboard becomes laggy. Read the proposed formulas, not just the layout.
