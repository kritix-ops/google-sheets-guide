# Fill with Gemini

Fill with Gemini is **UI sugar over the `=AI()` function** (covered in lesson 5.2): the same underlying model and the same data-handling surface, dressed as a one-click action instead of a formula. You select a range, trigger Fill (drag-fill or prompt-fill, the next paragraph spells out which is which), type a natural-language instruction, and Gemini proposes a column of literal values. You review the proposal, then apply or discard. The difference from `=AI()` is what lands in the cells: Fill writes static values, `=AI()` leaves a live formula.

It feels magic the first time. It also disagrees with itself between runs, occasionally fills cells outside your selection, and leaves no audit trail of what it changed. The Workspace Updates announcement claims 9x faster than manual entry on 100-cell tasks, and on a clean cleanup task that's the right ballpark. Used carelessly it introduces silent bugs the team finds two weeks later. This lesson is how to use it deliberately and where it beats both Smart Fill and an `=AI()` column.

## How it works in practice

Fill with Gemini has two invocation modes, and the entry point is different in each. Knowing which mode the team is in is half the lesson, because the prompt shape changes too.

| Mode | When | Entry point |
|---|---|---|
| **Drag-fill** | The column already has at least one completed seed cell | Hover the **bottom-right corner of the seed cell** and drag down. Gemini infers a pattern from the seed plus neighboring columns; no prompt typed. |
| **Prompt-fill** | The selection is empty | The **Fill button appears above the selection**. Click it, type a natural-language instruction, Gemini reads neighboring columns for context and fills the empty cells. |

Either way, the steps that follow are the same:

1. Gemini reads the *neighboring* columns of your selection to understand context. It shows you a proposal in a preview pane.
2. You review the proposal cell-by-cell.
3. You hit **Apply** to commit the literal values into the cells, or **Discard**.

The Apply step is what differs from `=AI()`. `=AI()` leaves a formula behind that recomputes on demand. Fill with Gemini writes static values once and walks away. Both have their place; the trade-off table below picks them apart.

## When Fill with Gemini beats Smart Fill and `=AI()`

Smart Fill (the older Sheets feature) learns from two or three example rows you type in. It works when the transformation is deterministic: extract a first name, capitalize a column, pull a country code out of a campaign name. If you can describe the transform with a regex or a `LEFT`/`MID` formula, Smart Fill probably does it too. `=AI()` is the live, formulaic equivalent. Fill with Gemini sits between them: smarter than Smart Fill, lighter weight than `=AI()`, and unlike both it writes literal values that don't depend on a recompute.

Fill with Gemini wins when the transformation is fuzzy:

- **Normalizing inconsistent text.** `"car deals pr"`, `"Car Deals PR"`, `"CARDEALSPR"` all collapse to `"Car Deals PR"` with a single instruction.
- **Classification of free text.** "Categorize this customer note as: complaint, question, compliment, other."
- **Multi-step transformations.** "Pull the city out, capitalize it, and standardize country codes to 2 letters."

Smart Fill needs examples. Fill with Gemini needs an instruction. When the rule is hard to demonstrate by example but easy to state in words, Fill with Gemini is the better tool.

| Task | Smart Fill | Fill with Gemini | `=AI()` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extract first name | Wins. Deterministic, free. | Overkill. | Overkill and billable. |
| Capitalize column | Wins. | Overkill. | Overkill. |
| Standardize 12 variants of "Car Deals PR" | Slow (needs many examples) and fragile. | **Wins.** One instruction, literal output. | Works but leaves a live formula that recomputes. |
| Classify customer notes (one-time clean) | Cannot do it. | **Wins.** | Works but the column drifts on each refresh. |
| Classify customer notes (ongoing) | Cannot do it. | Loses: literals freeze, won't update. | **Wins.** Live formulas track new rows. |
| Extract `card9999` from campaign name | Wins if format is consistent. | Wins too, but costs a Gemini call per row. | Same. |

The rule of thumb: if a formula or Smart Fill can do it, use those. Reserve Fill with Gemini for one-time cleanup tasks that need natural-language judgment. Reserve `=AI()` for ongoing classification.

## The proposal review step

The proposal panel is the most important and most-skipped step. The proposal shows you the cells Gemini would write. Always check three things before applying:

1. **Is every cell in the proposal what you expected?** Spot-check ten of them, especially the rows you'd never look at otherwise (rows 50, 137, 244, etc.).
2. **Did Gemini fill cells you did not select?** Occasionally the proposal extends beyond the original selection if Gemini decides additional derived columns are useful. Reject the apply if so.
3. **Did Gemini reorder rows?** Rare but real. The proposal preview usually keeps the original order; verify against a sibling column you didn't touch.

The Apply step is not always a clean single-undo unit. If you apply and then catch a mistake, `Ctrl+Z` may revert only some of the cells. Treat apply as commit-and-move-forward, not as a free preview. The team's rule: snapshot the workbook (File → Make a copy or version history) before any high-stakes Apply. Track 4 lesson 10 covers the named-version pattern in depth; the discipline transfers here unchanged.

## Cost, quota, and the privacy surface

Fill with Gemini is metered per Workspace plan. Some Workspace plans include promotional access windows with higher per-user quotas during the Gemini-in-Sheets rollout (also mentioned in lesson 5.1); verify with your admin which tier your team is on and what limits apply, since the gating shifts as features GA. Each Apply step counts against the quota. A 100-cell apply is one quota event in practice; a 600-cell apply is a bigger one. The 9x speed claim from the Workspace announcement is real for clean tasks, but the bill is real too. Watch it for high-volume cleanup runs.

PII has the same surface as `=AI()`: neighbor columns get read into Gemini's context, so if your selection sits next to a customer-email column, those emails are in the snapshot. Lesson 5.6 covers the PII policy in detail. The short version: if you wouldn't paste a column into an external vendor's web form, don't trigger Fill with Gemini on it without checking your plan's data-handling terms first.

## Used in the wild

The team faces this exact mess every week. The Taboola export sends vertical names in 12 different casings and three spellings. Until Fill with Gemini, the cleanup was a 20-minute weekly ritual: paste into the aggregator, run a `SUBSTITUTE` chain, manually fix the typos the substitutions missed. Now the workflow is:

- Paste the export into the staging tab.
- Select the Vertical column.
- Fill with Gemini, prompt: `Standardize each vertical name to Title Case and ensure it ends with PR. Allowed values: Car Deals PR, Bathroom Remodeling PR, Cruises PR, Hearing Aids PR, Online MBA PR, Dental Implants PR, Solar Systems & Panels PR, Senior Living PR, Pet Insurance PR, Cleaning Services PR, Roofing Services PR, Reverse Mortgage PR. For anything else, write 'needs review'.`
- Review the proposal. Apply.
- The remaining deterministic cleanups (whitespace trim, the date format) run through `TRIM` and `TEXT` formulas on the next column over.

The combination is faster than either tool alone: Gemini handles the fuzzy normalization, formulas handle the rest.

## Pro pitfalls

- **No formula trace.** The cells after apply look exactly like cells you typed. Two weeks later, nobody can tell which were AI-generated and which were manual. The fix: add a sibling column with the original value preserved, so a future reader can see the diff.
- **Lossy normalization.** If your prompt is "Standardize to canonical Title Case PR form" and Gemini sees "Senior Living P" (a typo), Gemini will guess "Senior Living PR" and may be right or wrong depending on whether the team has a "Senior Living" or a "Seniors" vertical. Always include the allowed list in the prompt if you have one, with an explicit "do not guess" rule.
- **Proposal that extends beyond your selection.** Gemini occasionally proposes derived columns alongside the main fill. The apply will write all of them. Inspect the preview carefully.
- **Apply is not a clean undo unit.** `Ctrl+Z` after apply may revert partially. Treat apply like a commit; save a snapshot of the workbook first if the stakes are high.
- **The "looks right but is wrong" trap.** Fill with Gemini's proposal looks confident. On a 200-row column, three rows being subtly wrong (a name capitalized inconsistently, a typo Gemini guessed wrong) is invisible at a glance. Spot-check 10 random rows in every apply, especially for fields that downstream `SUMIFS` or pivots will group on.
- **Header schema mismatch.** Abbreviated or ambiguous headers force Gemini to guess what your columns mean. The wrong guess is invisible in the proposal preview because the values look reasonable. Use full, unambiguous header names, and reference them in the instruction.
