# The 10M cell limit and what hits it

Google Sheets caps a workbook at 10,000,000 cells. Not 10 million *filled* cells. Ten million cells, period. The empty ones count. A tab that's been allocated as `A1:ZZ50000` burns 1.7M cells out of your budget before anyone types a digit. This is the limit that quietly kills aggregator workbooks: the team adds a platform, an IMPORTRANGE drags more rows than expected, somebody pastes a 200,000-row export onto an existing tab, and one morning Sheets refuses to insert another row.

The rule to internalize: **what counts isn't the data, it's the grid.**

## How Sheets allocates cells

When you open a new tab, Sheets gives it a default grid (currently 26 columns and 1000 rows = 26,000 cells). When you paste data wider or taller than that grid, Sheets extends the grid to fit. The grid never shrinks on its own. Delete the rows and the grid stays. Hide the columns and the grid stays. The only way to give cells back is to right-click the row or column header and choose Delete.

A few patterns that drift the budget without anyone noticing:

- **The paste that quietly extends.** Someone pastes a 50,000-row export onto `Raw - Taboola`. Sheets extends the tab from 1000 rows to 50,000 rows. Two weeks later they delete the data but the grid still has 50,000 rows.
- **The 40-column tab that uses 7.** Wide pivots and wide raw imports leave columns AA through AN allocated and empty. Each one is 1000+ cells (or however tall the tab is) sitting on your budget.
- **`ARRAYFORMULA` that's wider than expected.** A spilling `ARRAYFORMULA` extends the grid down to its furthest output cell. If the formula spills to row 100,000 because someone wrote `IFERROR(..., "")` over a column, the grid carries that to 100,000.
- **`IMPORTRANGE` without a bounded range.** `IMPORTRANGE(url, "Sheet1!A:Z")` pulls the full source sheet. If the source has 30,000 rows, your tab now has 30,000 rows too.

## Finding the waste

The cheap audit is two numbers on a tab:

```
=COUNTA(A2:lastRow)         filled cells in your useful payload
=COLUMNS(A:Z) * ROWS(A1:A1000)   allocated grid for this tab
```

Their difference is wasted cells. Their ratio is your efficiency. A tab where useful cells are 1% of allocated is a tab that's costing you 99% of its budget for nothing.

Run this across every tab. Sheets exposes the totals under **File > Settings > Spreadsheet stats** (cell count, formula count, IMPORTRANGE count). Anything over 5M cells is a workbook in the danger zone; anything over 8M needs an archive cycle this week.

## What happens when you hit the limit

The error reads "This action would increase the number of cells in the workbook above the limit of 10000000 cells." Common ways to trip it:

- Inserting rows. Even one row at a time.
- Pasting a block that would extend the grid.
- Adding a tab. A fresh tab with the default 26x1000 grid alone is 26,000 cells.
- An `IMPORTRANGE` that pulls more rows than fit. The cell shows `#REF!` with "Result too large" in the tooltip.

There's no soft warning. The workbook just stops accepting growth.

## Used in the wild

The team's aggregator workbook started at five platforms and seven tabs. Each tab was `A1:Z1000`. Total allocation: 7 * 26,000 = 182,000 cells. Two years later it had 14 platforms, 21 tabs, several with 50,000 rows from old IMPORTRANGE pulls that never got bounded, and one tab someone had extended to column ZZ for a one-off pivot in 2024. Total allocation: 4.8M cells. The fix wasn't a rewrite. It was an afternoon: delete the rows below the live data on each Raw tab, delete the columns past the schema, archive the 2024 months to a separate workbook, replace `IMPORTRANGE(url, "A:Z")` with `IMPORTRANGE(url, "A1:G5000")`. The workbook dropped to 900K cells and recalc time dropped from eleven seconds to under two.

## Pro pitfalls

- **Pasting into a column-locked range silently extends the sheet.** A protected range covers `A:G`, you paste a 12,000-row block in, and Sheets extends the tab down to row 12,000. The paste doesn't violate the protection (it's pasting into the allowed columns) but the grid grew.
- **`IMPORTRANGE` without a bounded second argument pulls the full source sheet.** If the source has a hidden 200,000-row archive, your aggregator just inherited it. Always bound: `IMPORTRANGE(url, "Sheet1!A1:G5000")`.
- **Charts that auto-extend their data range.** A chart pointed at `A:F` re-reads the entire column on every refresh. Combined with an IMPORTRANGE source, this is a budget and recalc time hit.
- **Hidden columns and hidden rows are NOT deleted.** They still consume cells. The audit catches them; the eye does not.
- **A new tab is 26,000 cells.** Twenty utility tabs that no one uses anymore is 520,000 cells of pure waste.
