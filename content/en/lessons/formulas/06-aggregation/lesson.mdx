# Aggregation: SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, COUNTIF, SUMIF, SUMIFS

The aggregators compress a range into a single number. The plain ones (`SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `COUNT`) are intuitive. The conditional ones (`COUNTIF`, `SUMIF`, `COUNTIFS`, `SUMIFS`) are the workhorses of every adtech dashboard: count campaigns matching a condition, sum revenue matching a condition, segment by buyer or platform or date range.

## The plain aggregators

The four functions every aggregation builds on. `SUM` and `AVERAGE` got their cards in lesson 3; `COUNT` and `COUNTA` are new here, and the COUNT-versus-COUNTA distinction is one of the most common silent-bug sources in adtech sheets.

`SUM(range)` adds. `AVERAGE(range)` averages. `MIN`/`MAX` find extremes. `COUNT(range)` counts numeric cells. `COUNTA(range)` counts any non-empty cell (numbers, text, anything).

## SUMIF and COUNTIF: one condition

The "IF" suffix lets you aggregate only the cells that match a condition. `COUNTIF(range, criterion)` counts matches; `SUMIF(range, criterion, [sum_range])` sums them. The criterion is a string with the comparison inside (greater-than, less-than, etc.) or a literal value.

In modern adtech workbooks the singular forms are mostly a stepping stone: once you understand them, you almost always switch to `COUNTIFS`/`SUMIFS` (next section) because filters in real reports are never single-criterion. The exception is a quick disposable "how many of X" check, where COUNTIF's two-argument shape is faster to type than COUNTIFS's pair.

`SUMIF` adds the optional third argument when the cells you sum are different from the cells you check. The classic case: "sum revenue for all of Yoav's campaigns": check column B for the buyer, sum column F for the revenue.

## SUMIFS and COUNTIFS: multiple conditions

The plural versions take **multiple** condition pairs. Notice the argument order is different from `SUMIF`: the sum range comes **first**, then alternating criterion-range and criterion pairs. This is the most-used Sheets function in business spreadsheets: every buyer-vs-platform pivot, every date-bounded report, every dashboard alert counter.

<FunctionRef name="SUMIFS" />

<FunctionRef name="COUNTIFS" />

```
=SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criterion1, criteria_range2, criterion2, ...)
=COUNTIFS(criteria_range1, criterion1, criteria_range2, criterion2, ...)
```

A typical adtech use: total revenue from Yoav's campaigns on Taboola only. Two criteria: buyer = "Yoav Cohen" AND platform = "Taboola".

## How to choose

| Goal | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Total a column | `SUM` |
| Average a column, ignoring text/blanks | `AVERAGE` |
| Count numbers in a column | `COUNT` |
| Count any non-empty cells | `COUNTA` |
| Count cells matching one condition | `COUNTIF` |
| Count cells matching multiple conditions | `COUNTIFS` |
| Sum cells matching one condition | `SUMIF` |
| Sum cells matching multiple conditions | `SUMIFS` |
| Average cells matching multiple conditions | `AVERAGEIFS` |

`COUNTIFS`, `SUMIFS`, and `AVERAGEIFS` are general enough that you can usually skip the singular versions entirely. If you remember one set, remember the plural. Modern dashboards are mostly `SUMIFS` and `COUNTIFS`.

## Used in the wild

In the team's actual workbooks:

- **The buyer-vs-platform pivot.** Rows are buyers, columns are platforms, cells are SUMIFS of revenue. The total row at the bottom is SUM of the column above, the total column on the right is SUM of the row to the left. Multiply this by 4 metrics (spend, revenue, profit, ROI) and you've got the team's standard weekly view.
- **The date-bounded summary.** Same SUMIFS, plus a third criterion: `>=` start_date AND `<=` end_date. The start/end dates live in named cells at the top of the dashboard ("current_period_start", "current_period_end") and the whole report updates when you change them.
- **The "alert" counters.** COUNTIFS for "campaigns with ROI < 0.5" or "campaigns with no spend in the last 7 days". These run at the top of the dashboard as a "things to look at" panel.

## Pro pitfalls

- **The SUMIFS argument-order trap.** Sum range first. Wrong order returns wrong numbers without erroring. Always always always.
- **Criteria strings need explicit comparison operators.** `SUMIFS(F:F, F:F, ">400")` works. `SUMIFS(F:F, F:F, 400)` matches only exact 400. `SUMIFS(F:F, F:F, "400")` is the same as exact match.
- **`SUMIFS(..., date_range, ">=2026-01-01")` doesn't always work.** Date strings as criteria are unreliable. Better: <code>SUMIFS(..., date_range, "{">"}=" {"&"} DATE(2026,1,1))</code> with concatenation.
- **Ranges of different sizes break SUMIFS silently.** Sum range and every criteria range must have the same dimensions. If one is `F2:F13` and another is `B2:B14`, SUMIFS returns wrong results without erroring.
- **Empty cells count as 0 in SUMIFS for numeric criteria.** If you have empty rows from IMPORTRANGE that haven't been populated yet, `SUMIFS(..., F:F, "{">"}0")` skips them; `SUMIFS(..., F:F, "{"<"}{">"}1000")` includes them as zeros. Watch for this in date-range filters too.
