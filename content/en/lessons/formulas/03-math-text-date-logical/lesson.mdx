# Math, text, date, and logical functions

Sheets is built from a small set of function families. Four of them cover most of what you'll write at work: math, text, date, and logical. This lesson tours the most-used members of each, gives every one its canonical reference card, and connects each to the adtech workflows it actually shows up in. Lookups (lesson 4), conditional logic in depth (lesson 5), and aggregation (lesson 6) build on top of what's here.

The grid model from lesson 1 (`$` locking, named ranges) and the array awareness from lesson 2 (when does a formula spill?) are both assumed. If a sentence references "the spill behavior of `FILTER`" or "the locked `$J$1`", flip back if needed.

## Math: SUM, AVERAGE, ROUND

The four operators (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`) work the way you'd expect. `^` is exponent. Wrapping operators with functions is what makes a spreadsheet a spreadsheet: `SUM` replaces twelve plus signs, `AVERAGE` replaces SUM divided by COUNT, `ROUND` replaces a manual truncation you'd otherwise re-do every quarter.

The everyday math functions are `SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `ROUND`, `ABS`, and `MOD`. The first three are in every workbook the team owns. `ABS` and `MOD` show up in narrower contexts (variance reports, banded row coloring), so we'll mention them in passing and leave the deep dive for lesson 23 (statistical) and lesson 11 (banding with `SEQUENCE` + `MOD`).

<FunctionRef name="ROUND" />

`ROUND` rounds to a given number of decimal places. `ROUND(91.234, 1)` is `91.2`. `ROUND(91.234, 0)` is `91`. Negative `places` rounds to tens, hundreds, etc. Used everywhere in adtech: revenue numbers presented to two decimals, ROI shown as a percentage, CPC bucketed to whole cents.

## Text: UPPER, LOWER, TRIM, SUBSTITUTE

Joining text uses the `&` operator: `="Buyer: " & B2` returns `Buyer: Yoav Cohen`. `CONCATENATE` exists, but `&` is shorter and more common.

The shaping functions are `UPPER`, `LOWER`, `PROPER` (case), `LEN` (length), `TRIM` (whitespace), and `SUBSTITUTE` (find-and-replace). The constant adtech use is normalizing values before they cross sheets via `IMPORTRANGE`: one upstream sheet has " yoav cohen ", another has "Yoav Cohen", and lookups silently fail until both sides are normalized. The deeper text manipulation lesson (lesson 7) covers `LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `MID`, `FIND`, `SEARCH`, and `TEXT`; for now we focus on the three reflexes you use every day.

`TRIM` is the single most useful normalizer in IMPORTRANGE-heavy work. Source sheets often carry stray spaces (leading from a CSV export, trailing from manual paste, doubled internal from CRM exports). The fix is one wrapper.

## Dates: DATE and the days-since-1899 model

In Sheets, every date is secretly a number: the count of days since `1899-12-30`. That's why date math just works. Subtract one date from another and you get the difference in days. Add 7 to a date and you get the same weekday a week later. The model has gotchas with imported text (CSV files give you dates as strings, not numbers), but for native cell entries the arithmetic is reliable.

`DATE(year, month, day)` builds a date from its parts. `TODAY()` and `NOW()` return the current date and the current date-plus-time. `EOMONTH`, `NETWORKDAYS`, and `DATEDIF` cover end-of-month, business days, and arbitrary date deltas; they get their full treatment in lesson 8. For now, knowing how to build a date and subtract two dates is enough to power the most common adtech use cases: campaign age, days remaining in a period, billing cycle boundaries.

## Logical: IF, AND, OR

The comparison operators (`=`, `<>`, `<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=`) compare two values and return `TRUE` or `FALSE`. They are the building blocks of every conditional column the team builds: "Is profitable?", "Above target ROI?", "Past end of period?", "Worth scaling?"

`IF` is the workhorse. The condition is anything that returns a boolean: a comparison, a function like `ISBLANK`, or another `IF`. When the conditions stack three or more deep, switch to `IFS` (lesson 5). When you need "all these AND together" or "any of these OR together", reach for `AND` and `OR`.

<FunctionRef name="AND" />

`AND` returns TRUE when every argument is TRUE. The classic adtech use answers "is this campaign both profitable AND high-volume". Both conditions must hold for the row to qualify for an aggressive scaling decision. `OR` is the dual: any one argument being TRUE is enough. `NOT` flips a boolean, mostly used inside `IF` conditions to invert an `IS-` check.

### Combined example: IF over an AND of two conditions

Layer `IF` and `AND` to produce a real scaling flag: "profitable AND spend at least $200".

## Used in the wild

In the team's actual workbooks, these basic functions show up like this:

- **`SUM` at the top of every column.** Total spend, total revenue, total clicks. Often wrapped in `ROUND` for a clean dashboard view. Often a `SUMIFS` (lesson 6) when you need to filter by buyer or platform first.
- **`TRIM` and `LOWER` on every join column.** Anywhere data crosses sheets (especially via `IMPORTRANGE`), normalize first or accept silent #N/A in your lookups. Lesson 4 shows the lookup-side version; lesson 27 covers `IMPORTRANGE` end-to-end.
- **`TODAY()` in date math for "days since" columns.** Combined with date columns from the source, gives you live-updating "campaign age" numbers. Watch out: `TODAY` recalculates daily, so values change every morning, and on a 50,000-row sheet it can hurt performance noticeably.
- **`IF` as the entry point to every conditional column.** "Is profitable?", "Above target ROI?", "Past end of period?". Lesson 5 covers when `IF` stops being enough and `IFS` takes over.

## Pro pitfalls

- **Don't include the header row in SUM/AVERAGE if the header could ever become numeric.** `SUM(E1:E13)` works only if E1 stays text. If someone renames the header to a number, you silently double-count.
- **`TRIM` doesn't fix inconsistent casing, and it doesn't strip non-breaking spaces.** If one source has "yoav cohen" and another has "Yoav Cohen", `TRIM` won't help; you need `LOWER` or `PROPER` on both sides. Non-breaking spaces (U+00A0) from Windows exports need a `SUBSTITUTE(text, CHAR(160), " ")` first.
- **`TODAY()` makes a sheet recalculate every day.** Fine for dashboards; expensive for a sheet with 50,000 rows that all reference `TODAY` indirectly. Lesson 28 (performance) covers the recalc cost in detail.
- **`=B2 + C2` errors silently if either cell is text.** Sheets returns `#VALUE!` or coerces unpredictably. Use `IFERROR` (covered in lesson 9) to catch.
- **`IF` with a non-boolean condition is a silent footgun.** Always make the comparison operator visible (`>`, `=`, `<`). Never rely on truthy-coercion.
